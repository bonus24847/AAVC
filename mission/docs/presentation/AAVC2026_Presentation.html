01 The Mission

Deliver four fragile eggs to the pads the committee names — autonomously

Six landing pads sit across the search area, each a 1×1 m white square with a 400 mm ArUco marker at its centre. The committee assigns four of the six to our team; the other two are decoys.

For every assigned pad the aircraft must fly the mandatory transit corridor, find the marker, land on the pad, and release that pad's egg — all within a single 20-minute operation window, with full marks for doing it without human intervention.

02 Concept of Operation

One flight, five phases — flown as a fixed sequence

Takeoff → P1·P2·P3 transit @ 20 m → boustrophedon search → land on pad, release after touchdown → egress transit → land & disarm

Transit

Mandatory corridor, both ways — scored per coordinate passed, in order.

Search

Sweep the search polygon at 10–20 m, decoding every pad it sees.

Deliver

Land on the pad; the egg is released only after touchdown confirms.

Return

Egress the corridor, land at Launch & Recovery, disarm.

03 System Architecture

Air and ground, one deterministic loop

  • The CM4 companion runs the whole mission deterministically — no cloud, no network, no LLM in flight (the rules ban internet/4G).
  • The Pixhawk 6X flies the aircraft and owns every failsafe; the single nadir camera is the sole control authority for finding pads.
  • The safety pilot's RC can retake the aircraft at any instant — the ground station monitors, it does not need to command.

05 Propulsion & Power Margin

Enough thrust in hand, and a pack that plans its own reserve

Six rotors hover the 7.2 kg aircraft at roughly 31 A, well inside their continuous rating. On five rotors PX4's allocator still holds attitude and thrust and gives up only yaw — recoverable by the safety pilot — and the airframe never approaches its limits in normal flight.

The 17 000 mAh pack gives a four-delivery flight a large reserve: the mission spends about 4.5 Ah of the 12.75 Ah usable, so a full sweep plus four landings finishes with the pack near 74%.

  • An energy-reserve policy refuses to start a flight — or a delivery — the budget cannot finish, so no egg is ever stranded on a dying pack.
  • The gauge is voltage-only and conservative: without current sensing it sags under load and reads low, so the 30 % return floor fires early — on the safe side. It did exactly that on 26 Aug, returning a pack on its third flight.

06 Finding the Pads

Classical computer vision — deterministic, no black box

Every pad carries a DICT_4X4_50 ArUco marker. We decode it directly with OpenCV — a fused check of the white-pad blob cue (a bright square with a dark centre) and the fiducial decode confirms both where a pad is and which id it is.

There is no machine-learning model anywhere in the flight loop — the same input always gives the same output. That is a deliberate choice: it is verifiable, it runs in a few milliseconds on the CM4, and there is nothing to mis-train.

  • At the 12 m sweep the 400 mm marker is only 28 px wide. Decode was measured at that size at every across-track offset to 94 % of the half-swath — no sweep spacing puts a pad where the detector cannot read.
  • Exposure is metered on the pad, not the grass. The driver's own AE clipped every white pad to 255 (2 788 in-flight frames, 0 decodes). A highlight-priority AE now holds the brightest 0.2 % of the frame at 190–225: the same afternoon the aircraft decoded 155 frames, four ids, from 8 m — the same 28 px KMITL gives at 12 m.

07 Precision Delivery

Land on the pad, release only after touchdown

The aircraft descends over the assigned pad in altitude rungs, re-centring on the marker at each step, until it is on the pad — final tolerance 0.2 m on a 1 m target. The egg hold opens only after touchdown is confirmed.

Two gates protect the egg and the score: the aircraft refuses to land unless the assigned id was actually decoded on the approach, and it never releases airborne. A wrong-id pad never steers the descent.

  • Height on the pad is judged against the home altitude latched at arming — not PX4's live home, which v1.17 rewrites in flight (we logged ±1–4.7 m on every real flight and neutralised it).
  • The autonomous descent rate is pinned at 0.4 m/s — a quarter of PX4's default — the rate every validated landing flew.

08 Autonomy & Tactics

Pad positions are unknown at takeoff — the aircraft discovers them

The committee gives us ids, not coordinates. So a flight whose pads aren't yet mapped flies a boustrophedon sweep of the search polygon, decoding every pad it sees into a registry keyed by marker id, confirmed by repeated votes.

Once the four are found, the aircraft serves them by the shortest route — an exact optimisation over the confirmed positions — not the order they were typed. On our test layout that cut the served route from 429 m to 266 m.

  • The sweep holds one heading, chosen so the camera's wide 1280 px axis lies across track — 5 legs at 12 m over the 226 × 73 m KMITL polygon. Leg overlap 0.30 was flown head-to-head against 0.44 at KMITL optics: same 6/6 pads and 4/4 eggs, 111 s faster.
  • Everything is rebuilt live, per flight; a later flight whose pads are already mapped skips the sweep and flies direct.

09 Safety & Redundancy

Three independent layers — the pilot always wins

1 · The safety pilot

Can retake the aircraft on the RC at any instant. The companion stands down the moment a takeover is seen — it commands nothing more.

2 · The flight controller

PX4 owns the failsafes: geofence → Return (the stored value verified, not just written), datalink-loss → Return, RC-loss → Return, low-battery → Return then Land, and a 50 m vertical fence as a gross-runaway net.

3 · The companion watchdog

An independent check on the 20 m ceiling and 10 m floor, no-fly zones, battery, GPS and telemetry staleness. If the FC ever returns or lands on its own, the companion stands down instead of fighting it.

  • Envelope protection is enforced, not hoped for: return altitude, vertical fence, descent rate and geofence action are pinned to the FC and read back to confirm they stuck; a 24-parameter board check gates every field day.
  • The ceiling lives in the companion for a reason: PX4 1.17 rewrites its home altitude for 120 s after takeoff (±1–4.7 m on every real flight we logged), so a home-relative FC fence is a random trigger — the watchdog reads AGL against the home latched at arming.
  • Proven on the aircraft, 21 Aug: the pilot-takeover drill on the real 6X — both detectors fired at the 1 s debounce and every command path was refused. Emergency mode is always available, as the DCOS requires.

10 Communication

One radio carries command, status and imagery — offline by design

A NOMAD ELRS-MAVLink link carries telemetry, vehicle status and the lidar stream between the aircraft and the ground station, with the coverage the rules ask for over the operation area.

Because the rules forbid a 4G SIM, the whole flight stack is offline: the mission runs on the CM4, and the ground console draws from the radio — a WiFi dropout can never freeze a panel or be mistaken for the aircraft.

  • Operating on the recommended bands: 920–925, 2400–2500, 5725–5850 MHz.
  • Imaging is recorded on board (5 Hz nadir frames) and relayed to the ground for the judges to follow.
  • Silent in the standby area: the pack is connected and the radio and console come up only at the launch point, as the rules require.

12 Field Operation

One console, one hand-off — a procedure for normal and emergency

At the field the operator opens the ground console, confirms the four assigned ids against the committee's card — shown as the marker picture, not just a number — and stages the flight.

Before the field day a 24-parameter board read proves the motor map, gauge, fence and height source are what we think they are. A pre-flight checklist then gates the launch: GPS matched to the flight controller, the aircraft inside the geofence, battery, link and RC all confirmed. A slide-to-confirm authorises the upload; then the safety pilot arms and flips to OFFBOARD, and the console goes green the instant the aircraft takes the mission.

  • Normal: stage → checklist → slide → arm → OFFBOARD → autonomous flight.
  • Emergency: the pilot flips to POSCTL — the companion stands down and the FC failsafes are the net.
